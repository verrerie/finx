# Return on Investment (ROI)

## Definition
Return on Investment (ROI) is a metric that evaluates the efficiency or profitability of an investment by comparing the gain from the investment to its cost.

## Formula
ROI = (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) * 100%

Where:
*   **Net Profit** = (Selling Price - Cost of Investment)
*   **Cost of Investment** = Initial cost of the investment

## Why It Matters
ROI helps investors understand the return generated from their capital. A higher ROI indicates a more efficient and profitable investment.

## Interpretation
-   **Good vs. Bad:** A positive ROI means profit; a negative ROI means a loss. What's considered "good" varies by industry, risk, and investment type.
-   **Industry Context:** Different industries have different typical ROIs. Comparisons should be made within similar contexts.

## Examples
### Stock Investment
-   **Scenario:** Buy stock for $10,000, sell for $12,000.
-   **Calculation:** Net Profit = $2,000. ROI = ($2,000 / $10,000) * 100% = 20%.

## Common Mistakes
1.  **Ignoring Time:** ROI doesn't inherently factor in the duration of the investment.
2.  **Incomplete Costs:** Failing to include all associated costs can inflate ROI.
3.  **Sole Focus:** Over-relying on ROI without considering risk or other factors.

## Related Concepts
-   **Return on Equity (ROE):** Profitability relative to shareholder equity.
-   **Net Profit Margin:** Profit generated per dollar of revenue.
